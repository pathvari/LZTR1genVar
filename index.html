How to read this dashboard

Each association is classified into one of four evidence tiers — from established cancer-risk syndrome (schwannomatosis) down to mechanistic or somatic-only evidence. Germline and somatic findings are kept distinct to avoid overclaiming cancer predisposition from tumor-sequencing data alone.

Most LZTR1 pathogenic variants have not been linked to a specific cancer risk estimate. Variant-level penetrance data is sparse; clinical management depends on the associated syndrome, not on individual variant calls.

Associated Conditions

LZTR1-Related Disease Spectrum

Three main germline conditions are linked to LZTR1 pathogenic variants. Only one — schwannomatosis — is an established cancer/tumor predisposition syndrome; the others carry uncertain or syndrome-level cancer risk.

Established

LZTR1-Related Schwannomatosis

Inheritance: Autosomal dominantPenetrance: Moderate / incomplete

Adult-onset tumor predisposition syndrome. Hallmark: multiple schwannomas (peripheral, spinal, cranial). Unilateral vestibular schwannomas reported. Typically presents with chronic pain. Median symptom onset ~30 years; diagnosis often confirmed after age 40.

Piotrowski et al., Nat Genet 2014 · Paganini et al., EJHG 2015 · OncoKB
Reported

Noonan Syndrome 10 (AD) & Noonan Syndrome 2 (AR)

Inheritance: AD (NS10) / AR (NS2)Penetrance: Variable

RASopathy with craniofacial features, short stature, congenital heart defects, developmental delay. Biallelic LZTR1 variants (NS2) described as carrying "a slightly increased risk for certain cancers." Across all Noonan syndrome genotypes, childhood cancer risk is estimated ~8-fold above population baseline, driven mainly by juvenile myelomonocytic leukemia (JMML).

NCBI MedGen NS10 · Motta et al., Hum Mol Genet 2019 · Demir et al., JCRPE 2025
Reported

Isolated Multiple Café-au-Lait Macules

Inheritance: Autosomal dominantFrequency in cohort: 6% (51/849)

Heterozygous LZTR1 loss-of-function variants identified in 6% of individuals with isolated multiple café-au-lait macules (CaLMs) lacking NF1/SPRED1 variants — an NF1-like phenotype. Truncating variants overlap with schwannomatosis- and Noonan syndrome-associated variants. In 2 families, CaLMs and schwannomas co-occurred.

Mastromoro et al., Genet Med 2024
Cancer Associations

LZTR1 and Specific Cancer Types

Germline LZTR1 variants are definitively linked to schwannoma/nerve sheath tumor predisposition. Evidence for other cancer types is emerging but remains limited — mostly case reports, pan-cancer bioinformatics, or mechanistic studies. Somatic LZTR1 alterations (frequent in glioblastoma) are reported separately from germline cancer risk.

Mechanism & Therapeutic Implications

Pathway Biology and Therapy Relevance

LZTR1 functions as a substrate adaptor for the CUL3 E3 ubiquitin ligase, targeting RAS-family GTPases and receptor tyrosine kinases (EGFR, AXL) for ubiquitin-dependent lysosomal degradation. Loss-of-function leads to RAS/MAPK overactivation and receptor kinase accumulation — creating therapeutic vulnerabilities that are still investigational, not standard of care.

EGFR + AXL Co-inhibition

Preclinical evidence (mouse models, cell lines) that LZTR1-mutant tumors accumulate EGFR and AXL and are vulnerable to concurrent inhibition of both receptor tyrosine kinases. Ko et al. (2023) demonstrated precision targeting opportunity for LZTR1-mutant cancers.

  • Mechanism: LZTR1 loss → impaired EGFR/AXL lysosomal degradation → kinase accumulation
  • Drug classes: EGFR inhibitors (e.g., osimertinib, afatinib) + AXL inhibitors (investigational)
  • Evidence level: Preclinical / mechanistic — no clinical trial data yet
  • Relevance: Somatic or germline LZTR1-mutant tumors (glioblastoma, other solid tumors)
Ko et al., Cancer Discov 2023

TKI Resistance in CML

Bigenzahn et al. (2018) identified LZTR1 in a haploid genetic screen for tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) resistance in chronic myeloid leukemia (CML). LZTR1 loss enhanced MAPK signaling and reduced sensitivity to imatinib and other BCR-ABL TKIs. Full-length LZTR1 re-expression reverted resistance.

  • Mechanism: LZTR1 loss → decreased RAS ubiquitination → enhanced MAPK signaling → TKI bypass
  • Domain specificity: Kelch-domain mutations disrupt imatinib sensitivity; BTB/BACK-domain mutations do not
  • Evidence level: In vitro / genetic screens — clinical correlation not yet established
  • Relevance: CML and potentially other TKI-treated malignancies
Bigenzahn et al., Science 2018 · Nature Chem Biol 2018 commentary

Osimertinib Resistance in NSCLC

A 2025 case report described an EGFR-mutated NSCLC patient harboring a germline LZTR1 c.1653C>G (p.Y551*) variant who had rapid disease progression on first-line osimertinib-chemotherapy (PFS 4 months vs. median 27.9 months). This is the first clinical report linking a germline LZTR1 mutation to EGFR-TKI resistance.

  • Variants: LZTR1 c.1653C>G (p.Y551*), heterozygous, classified pathogenic
  • Somatic correlates: LZTR1 stopgain R362* and R340* also identified in osimertinib-resistant NSCLC
  • Evidence level: Single case report + preclinical — insufficient for clinical action
  • Implication: Potential negative biomarker for EGFR-TKI efficacy; germline testing recommended
Park et al., Transl Lung Cancer Res 2025 · Oh et al., PMC 2022

MEK Inhibitors (Proposed)

Given that LZTR1 loss activates RAS/MAPK signaling, MEK inhibitors (e.g., trametinib) have been proposed as a therapeutic strategy for LZTR1-mutant tumors, particularly in the context of therapy resistance. This remains hypothetical, supported by pathway logic rather than clinical trial data.

  • Rationale: LZTR1 dysfunction → RAS accumulation → MAPK overactivation → MEK dependency
  • Drug class: MEK inhibitors (e.g., trametinib)
  • Evidence level: Mechanistic inference only — no clinical data
  • Relevance: Theoretical strategy for overcoming therapy resistance in LZTR1-mutant tumors
Wieleba et al., Int J Mol Sci 2025

Knowledge Gaps

What Is Not Known

Critical uncertainties in the current evidence base that prevent definitive cancer-risk estimation for individual LZTR1 variants.

Variant-specific penetrance: No numerical penetrance estimate exists for any individual LZTR1 variant. General penetrance is "moderate" and incomplete.

Non-schwannoma malignancy risk: Whether LZTR1 germline variants predispose to cancers beyond schwannomas/nerve sheath tumors is not established. Evidence is limited to case reports.

Therapy predictive value: No clinical trial data supports LZTR1 status as a predictive biomarker for any drug class. All therapy evidence is preclinical or from single case reports.

Genotype-phenotype correlation: No consistent correlation between specific LZTR1 variants and clinical phenotype (schwannomatosis vs. Noonan vs. CaLMs) has been established.

Population frequency paradox: The general-population frequency of potential LZTR1 LoF variants (0.36%) is substantially higher than expected for a pathogenic cancer-risk allele, complicating variant interpretation.

Germline vs. somatic distinction: Somatic LZTR1 mutations are frequent in glioblastoma (~22%) and other tumors, but this does not imply germline cancer predisposition. The relationship between germline and somatic LZTR1 alterations in oncogenesis remains poorly defined.
